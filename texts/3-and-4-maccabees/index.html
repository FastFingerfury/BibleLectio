Where the text comes from

3 and 4 Maccabees.

Brenton’s Septuagint · 1851 · 2 books, 25 chapters.

Two books of the Greek Bible that the King James Apocrypha does not have, written in Greek by Jews around the time of Christ. 3 Maccabees tells how Ptolemy IV of Egypt had elephants made drunk to trample the Jews of Egypt, and God sent two angels. 4 Maccabees is a sermon on reason ruling the passions, told through Eleazar, the seven brothers and their mother of 2 Maccabees 6 and 7.

Whose words.

The English of Samuel Bagster’s Greek and English Septuagint, printed in London in 1851 and known by Sir Lancelot Brenton’s name: the whole Greek Old Testament with an English translation beside it, these two books among its extra books. Public domain everywhere.

It is the classic English Septuagint, and its English is the old “thou” English of the app’s King James Apocrypha, close to the Greek word for word. The World English Bible’s 3 and 4 Maccabees are these words made modern by an editor. The other free English translation of both books is C. W. Emmet’s of 1918; Brenton’s was chosen over it as the classic, and its book also has the Psalm 151 of the app’s Psalms 151 to 155.

Where we read it.

  • ebible.org’s typed copy of the book (eng-Brenton), public domain, the words the app’s copy is made from
  • A Bagster reprint of the book of about 1900 on archive.org (septuagintversio1900bren): the printed pages 214 to 247, whose pictures were read by eye
  • A second scan of it (septuagintversio0000unse_g6n1)
  • A scan of another printing (brenton-lancelot-the-septuagint-with-apocrypha-old-testament-english-1851-impresso)

How the words were settled.

A typed copy of the book is free on ebible.org. We read it word by word against three scanned copies of the printed pages, and read every place where they differ on the page pictures. Ten places were put right as the book prints them: three verse numbers, “you” three times where the typed copy had “thou” or “thee”, “hades” three times without a capital, and one comma. The chapter and verse numbers are the book’s own.

The ten places, each read on the page pictures: three verses the typist numbered as parts of the verse before (“3a”, “6a” and “6b”, the book’s 4 Maccabees 10:4, 11:7 and 11:8); “you” where the typed copy had made it “thou” or “thee” (4 Maccabees 11:16, and 12:12 twice), the book having Brenton’s own mixture of “you” and “thou”; “hades” without a capital, as the book prints it (3 Maccabees 5:42, 5:51 and 6:31); and a comma the typed copy left out (3 Maccabees 4:11, “no communication with, his forces”). Everything else the scans read otherwise was the OCR misreading a letter. The book’s own spellings stay: “Philopater”, “catapeltæ”, “immoveable”.

The verse numbers.

The book’s own: 3 Maccabees, 7 chapters and 228 verses; 4 Maccabees, 18 chapters and 483 verses. Other editions number them alike but for a verse here and there: the World English Bible cuts 4 Maccabees 8:27 in two.

What is left out.

The Greek the book prints beside the English. Its italics, for the words the translator supplied, are not shown, as in the app’s King James Bible, and neither are its small capitals or its paragraphs.

The text itself.

brenton-1851.md: the file the app is built from: the two books with the book’s chapters and verses.
