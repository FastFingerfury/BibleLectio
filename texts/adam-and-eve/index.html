Where the text comes from

Adam and Eve.

S. C. Malan · 1882 · from the Ethiopic · 4 books, 141 chapters.

A Christian retelling of Adam and Eve’s life after Eden and of their line down to Christ, written in Arabic in Egypt some centuries after Christ and kept in Ethiopic. Four books.

Whose words.

S. C. Malan’s English of 1882, translated from the Ethiopic. Public domain everywhere.

Malan translated the Ethiopic, Dillmann’s text of 1853, with the Arabic beside it. His is the only complete English translation, and it is public domain everywhere (he died in 1894). Every later English “First and Second Book of Adam and Eve” is his words reworked: Rutherford Platt’s The Forgotten Books of Eden (1926) modernised a little and cut his paragraphs into numbered verses, and the apps and websites that carry the books carry Platt’s, which is public domain in the United States only. So the words here are Malan’s, with Platt’s verse numbers in Books 1 and 2 so that a place agrees with other apps’. Platt left out Malan’s Books 3 and 4, the same work going on from the flood to the Nativity; here they are, as 3 and 4 Adam and Eve.

How the words were settled.

No proofread digital copy exists, so we read the printed book: several scanned copies of the same pages, each word settled by the copies’ majority, and the doubtful places read on the page pictures. Books 1 and 2 carry Platt’s verse numbers of 1926, the ones other apps use. Books 3 and 4 were never cut into verses, so their numbers are ours, one sentence each.

No proofread digital copy of Malan’s text exists, so the words were taken from the printed pages, read by several copies and settled by vote. Books 1 and 2 (pages 1 to 142): two scans of the 1882 book and Platt’s reprint lined up word by word; where the two scans differ, Platt decides; a word the OCR broke is joined when Platt has it whole; a mark the OCR could not read is settled by what Platt has at that place; 119 slips mended by vote, 6 places where no two witnesses agreed read on the page, and 23 settlements made by reading the page pictures, among them four footnotes the OCR had let into the text and two exclamation marks it had read as a backslash. Books 3 and 4 (pages 143 to 208), which Platt did not print: six copies lined up with the first scan, the majority reading taken, with guards (a reading needs two votes; a word the app’s King James and other texts know is kept against a majority they do not); 50 slips mended by vote and 25 settlements read on the page pictures, among them a footnote the OCR had let in, the æ ligature the OCRs had lost (Sabæans, Chaldæans, Cæsar) and “son of Nun” where a broken letter read as t in every copy. The scripts, the hand settlements and the reports are kept with the text.

The verse numbers.

Books 1 and 2 carry Platt’s chapter and verse numbers of 1926 (79 and 22 chapters, 1,242 verses), the ones other apps use; Malan’s chapters are the same, and Platt’s verses fall at his paragraphs and sentences. Books 3 and 4 were never cut into verses by anyone, so their verses are ours: a sentence of Malan’s each, a sentence under six words joined to the one before (25 and 15 chapters, 783 verses). A verse number in them matches no other edition, there being none.

What is left out.

Malan’s footnotes, endnotes, brackets and chapter titles.

The text itself.

malan-1882.md: the file the app is built from: the four books, Malan’s chapter titles as headings, the scribe’s invocation before Book 1 in its preamble.
